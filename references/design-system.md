# Design System

## Visual DNA

The poster type is always a personal honor/achievement poster. Theme changes are allowed only inside this honor-poster structure; they should influence colors, symbols, props, motion, seasonality, and material style, but must not change the work into an e-commerce ad, event flyer, product poster, or generic campaign banner.

The user likes high-impact honor posters. Choose the visual language from the current month/theme instead of treating any single motif as mandatory. Common optional ingredients include:

- red-gold celebration palettes when the theme suits prestige, sales victory, or festive moments,
- 3D metallic titles and art typography when the theme calls for premium impact,
- flowing ribbons, light trails, and upward motion when the theme calls for sprint, celebration, or momentum,
- premium medal/photo frames,
- trophies, crowns, laurels, coins, stage lights, and controlled fireworks,
- 51Talk yellow-blue logo treatment,
- readable name/team/personal-title hierarchy.

Use dynamic references for movement, depth, and material richness only when they support the theme. Other themes may use cleaner business styling, summer color, sporty sky/blue motion, youthful pop visuals, or calmer premium layouts. Do not turn a personal honor poster into a crowded shopping advertisement.

## Non-Negotiable Honor DNA

Every poster must preserve these signals:

- a clear honor/achievement title,
- a congratulation or target-hit message,
- the supplied portrait as the central recognition subject,
- exact name, team, and personal title,
- 51Talk brand presence,
- Toki as a supporting branded mascot,
- campaign copy that praises performance rather than sells a product,
- theme elements used as decoration/support, not as the main poster category.

For example, `6月 618 销冠` should become a personal sales champion honor poster with restrained `618` cues, not a `618` discount promotion poster. `夏季冲刺` should become a summer sprint achievement poster, not a travel/music/event poster.

## Campaign Text vs Person Text

Always separate text into two layers of meaning.

Campaign-level text is generated from the month/theme and stays consistent for a campaign/month:

- main title,
- subtitle or theme strip,
- congratulation badge,
- sales motivational line,
- recurring visual motif,
- Toki role.

Person-level text changes per person:

- name,
- team,
- personal title such as `最佳...`,
- portrait.

Never use the personal title as the main campaign title. The personal title belongs near the name/team information area.

## ImageGen-First Principle

Use ImageGen to create the poster environment, frame system, title, theme-selected motion/material elements, Toki, and information zones as an integrated composition. The user's original portrait is a locked layer placed into the generated design; it is not regenerated by ImageGen unless the user explicitly asks.

Avoid this weak workflow:

1. Generate an empty background.
2. Paste the portrait into a blank circle.
3. Add flat rectangles and local fonts.

Prefer this workflow:

1. Inspect the portrait and decide the safest original-photo placement, without cutout or face alteration.
2. Prompt ImageGen for a complete honor-poster environment with a premium portrait window and a removable chroma-key placeholder (`#00FF00`) in the window.
3. Ask ImageGen to render short art text directly: main title, subtitle, theme strip, congratulation badge.
4. Remove the green window locally and place the original portrait underneath as locked pixels.
5. Add generated or code-rendered foreground overlays only if they help integrate the original photo without altering it.
6. Inspect aesthetic fit and revise the frame/environment if needed.

## Complexity Budget

Use fewer elements with more depth. A good honor poster usually needs:

- 1 hero title system,
- 1 portrait feature,
- 1 Toki moment,
- 2 to 3 theme symbols,
- 1 to 2 motion systems selected by theme, such as ribbon, light trail, upward rays, waves, sport motion, clean spotlight, paper texture, or graphic rhythm.

For 618/e-commerce themes, choose only 2 to 3 of these: `618 numerals`, crown/trophy, coupon/ribbon, subtle gift box, sales chart, spotlight, coins. Do not include every symbol.

If the poster starts to look like a promotion ad, remove shopping clutter and restore the honor hierarchy: title, portrait, name, personal title, team, congratulation, motivational line.

## Layout Logic

Default vertical hierarchy, with visual freedom:

1. Clean brand-safe area in the top-left zone for the official logo asset.
2. Main art title.
3. Month/theme strip or subtitle.
4. Portrait in medal/card/hero frame.
5. Congratulation badge.
6. Name.
7. Personal title.
8. Team.
9. Motivational line.

When same-month unified layout is `是`, keep the generated composition system stable for the month: same camera language, title treatment, portrait feature, Toki role, and information rhythm. When `否`, vary composition by theme and person.

## Portrait Window Design

The photo window must be designed for the supplied portrait.

- For close-up selfies, use a vertical champion card, magazine-cover frame, beveled glass panel, or trophy-screen window. Avoid forcing the selfie into a circular medal if it crops the head badly.
- For half-body studio portraits, a tall arch, stage card, or gold medal frame may work.
- For full-body portraits, use a larger vertical frame or poster-screen window.
- The generated window should have a clear transparent/chroma-key region that matches the expected photo shape.
- Foreground rims, ribbons, or light effects may overlap the photo edge but must not cover the face.

## ImageGen Usage

Use ImageGen for:

- whole-poster key visual,
- portrait-window, frame, or medal design around the original photo,
- art typography for short important text,
- 3D materials,
- theme-selected motion/material elements such as ribbons, light trails, trophies, coins, seasonal motifs, sporty graphics, clean spotlight, or youthful pop shapes,
- Toki theme pose when proportions are explicitly constrained.

Avoid relying on ImageGen for:

- recreating or modifying the supplied person's face/body,
- long dense text,
- small footer information,
- multi-line team/person details that must be exact,
- dense Chinese or English details.

For short text, prefer ImageGen art typography inside the whole poster. If exact text is wrong, try a localized ImageGen edit first. If local text correction is needed, match the generated material, lighting, shadow, outline, and perspective.

## Portrait Preservation Rules

- The supplied portrait must remain the real photo, not an AI-recreated person.
- Do not use ImageGen to redraw the face, reshape the body, change hair, change clothing, change pose, or beautify the person.
- Do not remove the photo background or cut out the person unless the user explicitly requests cutout.
- Prefer placing the original photo inside a premium generated photo window: medal frame, arch frame, trophy frame, glass frame, ribbon frame, stage screen, or layered card.
- The generated frame may overlap photo edges with ribbons, glow, borders, trophies, or foreground effects, but must not cover the face or key pose.
- Allowed local operations: proportional scale, safe crop, slight color/brightness matching, and mask to the generated photo-window shape. These are for presentation only, not identity alteration.

## Text Hierarchy

- Main title: ImageGen art typography, largest, visually dominant.
- Subtitle/theme: short, campaign-level, readable.
- Name: second-largest exact text, high contrast.
- Personal title: strong, readable, usually gold or white-gold, but below name/title hierarchy.
- Team: clean, smaller but clear.
- Motivational line: one or two lines max.

If copy is too long, rewrite it shorter before shrinking.

## 51Talk Logo

Use `assets/51talk-logo-reference.png` as the default brand asset. It is a rounded transparent PNG made from the user's provided official logo, so composite it directly instead of redrawing it from memory. `assets/51talk-logo-original.png` is only a backup/reference for the uncropped rectangular version.

ImageGen should not create the logo. Prompt ImageGen to leave a clean empty top-left brand-safe area only. Do not ask ImageGen to render any logo, brand text, yellow logo badge, or `51Talk` wordmark. The official asset is added once in local compositing after the generated poster frame is ready.

Place the composited logo in the top-left brand zone by default, consistent with the user's poster habits. Keep enough safe margin from the poster edge and title. The logo may sit on the poster background as a clean rounded badge, but it should not look like a sharp pasted rectangle.

Do not center the logo by default. Only use a centered logo when the user explicitly requests it, or when continuing an approved same-month master layout that already fixed the logo there.

Avoid any double-logo workflow: if ImageGen accidentally draws a fake logo, remove/regenerate that region or cover it cleanly before placing the official asset. Do not leave a ghosted generated logo under the official one. Do not invent extra official taglines unless the user provides them.
